Visualizing the Central Limit Theorem

Instructions

The Central Limit Theorem (CLT) states that the sampling distribution of the standardized sample mean from a large number of independent, identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables will be approximately a Standard Normal Distribution, \( \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \), regardless of the original population's distribution.

  • We begin with a randomized "True Population Distribution" with population mean \(p\) and standard deviation \(\sigma_p\).
  • Each "Sample" consists of \(n\) i.i.d. draws from this population, from which we calculate a sample mean, \(\bar{x}\).
  • We then standardize this mean to get a Z-score: \( Z = \frac{\bar{x} - p}{\sigma_p / \sqrt{n}} \).
  • The large chart plots a histogram of these Z-scores. As you draw more samples, observe how this histogram converges to the overlaid red Gaussian curve.
  • Notice how the Standard Error (\(\sigma_p / \sqrt{n}\)) decreases as the sample size \(n\) increases.
